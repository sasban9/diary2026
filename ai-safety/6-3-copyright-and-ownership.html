Module 6 · Lesson 3

When AI creates something, who owns it?

You?The AI company?The artists it learned from?Nobody?

Part A: what you put in. Part B: what comes out.

Part A · What you feed it

Before you feed it, check who owns it.

  • Your own writing, photos and notes
  • Material you’re licensed or allowed to use
  • Others’ articles, books or paid reports: check the licence and the tool’s terms
  • Photos or voices of real people: get consent
  • Confidential or NDA material (lesson 6.2)
  • Pirated copies or paywall workarounds

Real-world example

Courts and governments are still deciding.

Nov 2024: ANI sues OpenAI in Delhi→ Dec 2025: a government panel proposes a mandatory licence with royalties→ Jul 2026: the court declines an interim injunction

The proposal isn’t law yet and the case continues. Similar lawsuits are running in other countries. Until it’s settled, don’t assume anything online is free to feed in.

Part B · What comes out

No human author, no copyright.

AI only

Not protected by copyright in the US.

AI + your creative edits

You can own the parts you created, selected or arranged.

Prompts alone

Not enough, in the US Copyright Office’s current view.

The US Supreme Court declined to hear Thaler v. Perlmutter on 2 March 2026, leaving the human-authorship rule in place. Other countries differ.

A common mix-up

Terms can give you use, not exclusive rights.

The tool’s terms · a contractUsually say you may use the output, sometimes commercially.
≠
Copyright law · a right against everyoneOnly protects human-authored parts.

So others may be free to copy purely AI-made work. India’s Copyright Act has a clause on computer-generated works, but how it applies to generative AI isn’t settled.

Your checklist

Before you publish.

Check the tool’s terms: is commercial use allowed?
Look for lookalikes: run a reverse image or plagiarism check
Don’t imitate living artists, brands or real people’s faces and voices
Add real creative work of your own, and keep a record
Disclose where a platform or client requires it (lesson 6.1)

Hands-on · 8 minutes

Run an ownership audit.

  1. 1Pick something you made with AI. Label it: AI-only, AI-assisted, or human-led.
  2. 2Read your tool’s terms on output ownership and commercial use.
  3. 3Run a lookalike check, and write down what you personally contributed.
