Know what to look for

A good policy answers six questions.

Q1

Which AI tools am I allowed to use?

Q2

What data must never go in?

Q3

Who checks the output before it’s used?

Q4

When must I tell clients or customers?

Q5

Who owns what I create?

Q6

What do I do if I slip, and who do I tell?

If there isn’t one

No policy? That isn’t permission.

Ask your manager or IT→ Assume the strictest rule→ Write down what you do→ Suggest a simple policy

Until you hear otherwise: approved tools only, nothing confidential in, and a human checks every output.

What works

Bans alone push use underground.

Ban only

  • Use goes underground
  • Staff turn to personal accounts
  • Leaders lose visibility

Clear rules + approved tools + training

  • Use is visible and discussed
  • Data stays in approved tools
  • People know what’s allowed

The same KPMG study found 57% of workers hide their AI use from supervisors.

Hands-on · 10 minutes

Read it. Question it. Improve it.

  1. 1Find your workplace’s AI policy and read it end to end.
  2. 2Mark three points that are unclear, and ask your manager about them.
  3. 3No policy? Draft a one-page “team AI rules” using the six questions.
