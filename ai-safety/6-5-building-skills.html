Bastani et al., PNAS 2025: nearly 1,000 Turkish high school students learning maths. Students leaned on the plain chatbot as a crutch.

Why it happens

The answer feels like learning. The struggle is learning.

Feels like learning

  • Reading a perfect answer
  • Copying a clean solution
  • Everything making sense as you read

Is learning

  • Struggling to recall, then checking
  • Explaining it in your own words
  • Getting it wrong, then fixing it

In the study, students using the plain chatbot didn’t realise they were learning less.

The habit

You first. AI second.

You: attempt, notes, a guess
AI: feedback
Write your own attempt before you ask
Say what you already know and where you’re stuck
Ask for a hint, not the answer

Part B · AI as coach

Make it ask you questions.

“Don’t solve it. Give me a hint for the first step only.”
“Ask me questions until I can explain it myself.”
“Here’s my attempt. Point out one mistake, don’t fix it.”
“Quiz me on this topic, one question at a time.”

Real-world example

Same AI, different prompt, different result.

The crutch
“Solve this for me.”
+48% practice, −17% exam
The coach
“Hints only. Don’t give the answer.”
+127% practice, no exam penalty

The only big difference was whether the AI handed over answers or guided the student to them.

Choose what to keep

Protect the skills you can’t afford to lose.

Writing your own argumentEstimating and checking numbersReading closelyThe core craft of your jobMaking judgement callsRemembering key facts

A test: if AI were down tomorrow, which skills would you miss? Those are the ones to keep practising by hand. Automate the rest.

Hands-on · one week

Run a skills experiment.

  1. 1Pick one skill you want to keep sharp.
  2. 2For a week, make a ten-minute first attempt yourself before using AI on it.
  3. 3Switch to coach prompts. At the end, do one task with no AI and see how you do.

Back to our question

Used as a crutch, it weakens you. Used as a coach, it can strengthen you. Try first.
