The first-time parents’ toolkit

For couples, from before you conceive to your child’s early years. Built from patterns that kept repeating in mothers’ accounts of late-pregnancy complications, stillbirth and children’s long-term conditions.

1 · The “first-timer” labelConcerns get discounted, and you have no baseline to compare.
2 · “Normal” isn’t “all clear”One good scan or trace can close the conversation too early.
3 · The boring explanationItch, headache, nausea get put down to normal pregnancy.
4 · Reassured, no return planSent home without clear instructions on when to come back.
5 · Nobody owns the follow-upTests ordered, but no one responsible for the result.

08If you’re being brushed off

Say it calmly, say it clearly, and bring your partner.

“My baby’s movements have changed from their usual pattern. I need to be monitored today.”
“What exactly are we ruling out? Was the heart trace normal by your unit’s definition? Were growth and fluid checked?”
“Before I leave: which signs mean I come straight back, and can I have that in writing?”
“I’d like a senior doctor to review this, and my report noted in my record.”
Drawn from roughly 60 mothers’ accounts, a regulator’s case file, and guidance from RANZCOG, ACOG, FIGO, FOGSI, USPSTF, RCOG and Ireland’s HSE. A sample, not a statistical study. This is education, not medical or legal advice, and not every failure is negligence; guidance varies by country, so confirm every item with your own doctor.
